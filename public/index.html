How this week works

Five days, five brand-new timed projects. Each day the instructor starts the clock; you build, you submit before the buzzer, and the AI grading agent scores your submission across six categories. You are graded against the brief — not against each other.

The grading agent & its brains

The grading agent runs on the brain the instructor picks — Mistral, NaraRouter or UnoRouter (or auto, which tries them in order until one answers). It reads every submission against the day's brief and caveats, then scores Goal, Craft, Creativity, Technical and Process. Time Discipline is computed by the server from the clock — never by the AI. It grades what you paste, so paste your prompt logs and descriptions. When the instructor switches on the Vision pass, a separate vision model also reads your uploaded images and adds what it sees as extra evidence — but your pasted work still counts most, and the human instructor reviews attached files and can adjust any score.

Video days (Day 3 & Day 5)

Day 3 (The 30-Second Sell) and Day 5 (Launch Day) ask for a video. This app takes images, not video files — so on video days you paste the storyboard, the voice script, every prompt you used, the exact duration, and a link or full path to the MP4. Attach a thumbnail so the vision pass can see a frame. The AI grades what you paste; the instructor watches the actual clip. No AI here can watch video — your written evidence is what earns the Process and Goal scores.

The golden rule

Proof of steps, not vibes. Every submission needs its evidence pasted in: the prompts, the traces, the logs. No evidence = low Process score, whatever the result looks like.

Make-up windows

Let a latecomer complete a closed day without touching the class clock or re-running anyone. Open a window per student; their Time score is excused (no late penalty) when you tick that box.

Time your students live only when they are actually in the room. The timer starts when you press Start clock in the Console — it stamps exactly the students who are logged in at that moment. Late sign-ins are stamped when they next log in, and your Logged-in counter shows who is online right now.

Cloud backup — your safety net

Every submission, grade and the clock are saved to a private GitHub repo within seconds of any change, plus a safety sweep every 5 minutes. This free server wipes its disk on every restart — the cloud backup is what saves student work, automatically.

Restore overwrites the current state with the latest cloud snapshot — use it after a server wipe, or to undo a mistake on a shared machine.
